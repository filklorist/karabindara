TO: Deputy Chief of Office & Commander of Land Forces, Office for Conducting War in the Western Gulf
FROM: Tala Sibala, Principle Secretary, Office for Conducting War in the Western Gulf

I am gladdened to have heard of your wife's successful pregnancy and I look to learning abut your new child. That you have arrived now in Zaroon to take command of our just-landed forces is of course also gladdening.

These forces, at the moment, consist of the 2nd Provisional Brigade. This includes the veteran but somewhat understrength 28th Naval Infantry Regiment, three fresh regiments of foot, a light infantry regiment, and twenty-eight guns. In addition to those regiments of Regulars and Conscripts, a group of mercenaries under self-styled "General" Sprambis Rose have been contracted by private forces on behalf of the king to supplement our efforts. This mercenary band has been quite controversial around The Office in part due to their aggressive attempts to recruit for this expedition. I myself recieved a letter shortly before setting off from Cisani opening with the couplet:

"Come all ye fillibusterers and roving buccaneers, ye raparees and picaroons and wayward privateeers, ye gentlemen of fotrute, roaring captains one and all! Come join the tale of Gen'ral Rose and how he'll save us all!" Although some of my colleagues were appalled that an officer of the service should seemingly be sent the same letter as a common brigand, I found this wondrous despite the rhyme of "all" with itself. As for the soldiery under "Gen'ral" Rose, I would not trust them to do the work of a line regiment with the professionalism expected of such a unit. Rose himself has proven to be incredibly fortunate in military matters and he is a sharp fellow so you will surely find use for him and his compatriots.

The enemies in the immediate vicinity of Zaroon are few. Forts Sands and Collum are reduced nearly to rubble by our mortar schooners, though the ruins of Fort Collum I understand still holds some stragglers refusing to hoist the white flag. Enemies within the city are certainly many, however. Your compatriot in command of the naval forces asigned to this expedition has been conferred acting governorship of the city and occupied lands in the region, and as such those affairs are not strictly your responsibility.

Questionably reliable information indicates that the routes out of Zaroon are well defended, despite the surprise with which we took the city itself. We are told that the Red Line railroad's crossings of both the Talid and Kar are rigged for demolition, that Fort Harfington is a sturdy and relatively modern stronghold of thirty-some-odd guns and a regiment-strength garrison, that another full regiment has rushed to guard the bridge over the Kar, and Fort Corg of Rictor Island and Kibroz beyond are hotbeds of religious fanatics willing to put up more fight than perhaps anywhere else in the Kara'Bindara.

This intelligence has been conveyed to us by members of the anti-royalist intelligentsia that frequent Zaroon's Salon District, though from whence these rumors come originally is harder to discern. Scouting parties of the 102nd have been dispatched to confirm or deny this, and are yet to meet any resistance to our south or west other than angry boaters throwing rotten fish. They do report bivouacced soldiers across the Warsh, but have not provided an estimate of strength. Mr. Doas, the Chief of Engineers of the 2nd Provisional Brigade, has gone to survey the defenses at Harfington, but no report has yet crossed my desk.

Remember well our goals in this land: a hasty end from a hasty start at the tail of a long, plodding war. Great heaps of coal and timber certainly flow down the River Kar and Zaroon is doubtlessly amongst the most important cities of Lacron, but these people may well fight broken-backed and fruitlessly for years if we can not bring about the falls of the great trade hub of Goobinschtyle and the Royal City of Brimisora.
